cannot evaluate the business, and showing income figures to someone with no product
experience is how you end up with a partner who oversells.

## Objections

Record real ones as you hear them, with what actually worked — not what should have.

### "Isn't this one of those pyramid things?"

Answer it directly; deflecting confirms the suspicion.

> "Fair question. The difference is where the money comes from — this pays on product
> actually sold to customers, not on signing people up. You can look at the compensation
> plan yourself, and I'll show you the income disclosure so you can see what people
> actually earn, including the people who earn nothing."

Then **show both**. The willingness is the answer.

### "I don't have time"

Usually means "I don't believe the return justifies the time." Do not fight it — find out
which. If it is genuinely time, a partner role may fit where a rep role does not.

### "I don't want to sell to my friends"

Legitimate and common. Explore whether the product-first path works for them: they use it,
people ask, they answer. If they still do not want it, they are not a fit and that is fine.

### "How much do you make?"

Answer honestly or decline honestly. Never inflate. If your numbers are small, saying so
plainly costs you this prospect and earns you every future one.

# Rep Conversation — NEPQ Structure

For talking to someone who might **build** the business, not just buy a unit.

NEPQ works because it never argues. You ask; they conclude. The moment you start
convincing, you have taken responsibility for a decision that has to be theirs, and they
will hand it back to you the first time it gets hard.

Ratio to hold yourself to: **they talk 70%.**

---

## Stage 0 — The PFAS opener

Water is the rare product where the prospect already has the problem and can be shown it.
Open there, not on the business.

> "Did you see the reporting on PFAS — the 'forever chemicals' — showing up in tap water?
> … Have you ever looked up what's actually in yours?"

Then: **let them look it up themselves.** Handing someone their own municipality's water
report is more persuasive than any claim you can make, and it costs you nothing in
credibility because you did not say it — the report did.

> **Constraint:** stay inside what eSpring's certifications actually state. See
> `../../context/espring_product.md`. Do not extrapolate from a news story to a claim about
> what the unit removes.

---

## Stage 1 — Connection

Lower the stakes. You are not selling yet, and they can feel whether that is true.

- "How long have you been in [their work]?"
- "Is that what you pictured doing?"

## Stage 2 — Situation

Neutral fact-finding. No leading.

- "What does a normal week look like for you right now?"
- "Where does your water come from — city, well?"

## Stage 3 — Problem awareness

Move from facts to how they feel about the facts. This is where the conversation turns.

- "When you saw that report — what went through your mind?"
- "Has the money side of things been where you want it, or is there a gap?"
- "What have you already tried about that?"

## Stage 4 — Solution awareness

Let them describe the fix. What they articulate, they own.

- "If you could change one thing about that, what would it be?"
- "What would need to be true for you to feel like it was handled?"
- "How much time a week could you realistically put toward it — honestly?"

*(That last answer is the `hours` input in the calculator. Use their number, not ten.)*

## Stage 5 — Consequence

Gentle. Once, not repeatedly. Twice is pressure and they will feel it.

- "If nothing changes in the next two years, where does that leave you?"

## Stage 6 — Qualifying → the calculator

Only now do numbers appear.

> "Can I show you how the plan actually pays? I want to walk you through the math rather
> than just tell you a number — and I'll show you the assumptions it's making, because
> they matter more than the total."

Open `tools/espring_income_calculator.html`. Then:

1. **Set hours to *their* number** from Stage 4.
2. Show **Just you** first. Ten units a month, what that pays. Let it sit.
3. Show **You + team**. Then immediately explain *why* it is ~2.8× and not 11× — their
   team keeps their own bracket, you earn the spread, group volume lifts your bracket.
4. Open the breakdown. Show that most of the solo number is retail margin — real work,
   real sales.
5. **Name the assumptions before they do.** All six, from
   `../../context/compliance_and_income_claims.md`.
6. **Hand them the Income Disclosure Statement.** Every time. No exceptions.

The person who says yes after step 5 and 6 is worth ten who say yes before them.

## Stage 7 — Commitment

- "What's your honest read on it?"
- "What would you need to know to decide either way?"

Do not close on the first meeting. Ask what they want to look into and set the next
conversation.

## Onboarding a new rep — first 30 days

*(Align this with the Yeager Group system — see `../../context/yeager_group.md`.)*

**Week 1**
- [ ] Registration complete
- [ ] Their own eSpring installed — nobody sells a unit they do not use
- [ ] Walked through this file and the compliance file
- [ ] First conversation list built (no pressure to contact yet)

**Week 2**
- [ ] Plugged into system training
- [ ] Sat in on one of your conversations as an observer
- [ ] Can explain PV vs BV correctly

**Week 3**
- [ ] Ran a demo with you present
- [ ] Verified their own copy of the calculator rates
- [ ] Can name all six assumptions unprompted

**Week 4**
- [ ] First independent conversation
- [ ] Debrief — what happened, not what should have
- [ ] 90-day expectations set honestly, in writing

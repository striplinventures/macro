# Brand Voice

## How we sound

**Plain, specific, and unhurried.** The product is expensive and the business is a real
commitment. Anyone who can be rushed into either will be gone in ninety days, so urgency
tactics cost more than they earn.

- Short sentences. Concrete nouns. Real numbers.
- Curious before persuasive — questions outnumber claims.
- Comfortable naming the downside. It is the fastest way to be believed.

## What we never say

- "Guaranteed", "passive income", "risk-free", "financial freedom in X months"
- "It's only ten a month" — it is not *only* anything; ten high-ticket sales a month is work
- Any income figure without the Income Disclosure Statement in hand
- Anything about water contaminants beyond what the certifications actually state
- "Most people fail because they're lazy" — most people stop because the math was
  misrepresented to them at the start

## The tone test

Before saying it, ask: **would this sentence embarrass me if the prospect's accountant
heard it?** If yes, cut it.

## On presenting income

The temptation is to lead with the biggest number. Resist it. The number that survives
scrutiny is the one you walked someone through — showing that ten people selling ten is
worth about **2.8×** one person selling ten, not eleven times, is more persuasive than
the headline figure, because it proves you are describing the plan rather than selling a
fantasy.

Lead with the mechanism. Let the number land second.

## Words that work

| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| "Opportunity" | "Business" |
| "Get in" | "Start" |
| "Downline" | "Team" (with prospects; "downline" is internal) |
| "You'll make" | "The plan calculates" |
| "Passive" | "Recurring, once the work is done" |
| "Easy" | "Simple, but not easy" |

## The mentorship — this leads, always

While I was knocking doors for LeafGuard I got connected to an entrepreneurial
mentorship group — people who actually mentor you on how to achieve financial
freedom. About forty years ago, my mentor and his wife walked away from their
jobs at thirty-one and thirty years old. Since then they've had the freedom to
travel the world, mentor other people, and be involved with what they actually
care about.

That spoke to me. Having the freedom to travel is something I'd always imagined
for myself, so I wasn't going to let the opportunity pass. I started bugging the
guy, asking if he'd be willing to mentor me. I had to chase him down before he
accepted me as a mentee.

This is the part that opens every conversation. The water business, the sales
job, the dropping out — that's background, and it only comes up after.

## What made me open to it

I started pursuing entrepreneurship my senior year of high school, mostly
because I didn't really know what I wanted to do.

When I got to college I realized I didn't connect with a lot of the people
there — they just weren't entrepreneurial. And I didn't see a way to actually
get started in business from business school at my local community college. So I
dropped out and went into sales at LeafGuard.

What I found in sales was a glass ceiling. You can only go so far.

## What I actually got out of it

When I first got started I thought it was going to be an automatic rocket — that
I'd blow up and be a millionaire overnight.

The real value has been how much I've grown, and how long I've had to deny
myself things I wanted. Travel. Certain vehicles — Porsches.

The mentorship is the biggest piece. It's like Rocky: I've got Mickey in my
corner. Someone I can reach out to and ask questions, who can coach me and guide
me because he's already been there. He's been in the fight, and now he's able to
coach.

## Why water

I got inspired by the movie *Erin Brockovich*, because I found out that local
carpet companies in my area were dumping chemicals into local water so the carpet
would be waterproof. They didn't let people know what those chemicals were doing
to our bodies, or that they were going into local water sources.

I saw a need in my own community, and I saw that I had the ability to fill it
through my business. That's why I pursued water filtration.

## Where it goes

I now see avenues where I can help other people start their own businesses
through water filtration. And if they want to be mentored, I can help them there
too — if they want to truly grow a cash flow asset, and not just a business or a
side hustle.

## How this maps to the tracks

The story isn't decoration; it's the reason the three departments exist.

- **The glass ceiling in sales** is why the rep track exists — a way for someone
  already selling to earn on something that isn't capped by a commission
  structure.
- **Chasing down a mentor** is why the partner track gates hard. I had to prove I
  was hungry before I was accepted. That's the pattern, run the same way.
- **The carpet mills and the water** are why the customer track leads with
  education. People weren't told what was in their water. We tell them.

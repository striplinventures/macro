# Skill: Partner Qualification (Yager 8-Step Pattern)

**Use this when:** evaluating, advancing, or writing anything for a partner
candidate. Not for rep-track people — see `../../reps/skills/sales_script.md`.

**Done looks like:** the candidate has moved exactly one step, the step is
logged, and the next step has a date on it. Not "had a good conversation."

## The pattern

1. Desires & Goals
2. Commitment
3. List
4. Connecting / MG1 / MG2
5. Business Overview (OV1)
6. Follow Through / OV2 / FT1 / FT2 / Launch
7. Review Progress
8. Rotate Pattern

Never skip a step to move faster — the pattern **is** the qualification filter.
A candidate who won't sit through steps 1–3 isn't a partner candidate yet; route
them to the rep track instead (see `../../reps/`).

## Gating material

- *Business of the 21st Century* — Kiyosaki
- *The Go-Giver*

The gate is whether the candidate actually engages with these, not whether they
say yes to reading them. Ask what they took from a specific chapter; a candidate
who can't answer hasn't opened it, and the answer to that is time, not pressure.

## Objection handling — "I need to think about it"

1. Reframe as helping them think it through, not pressuring them.
2. Ask what specifically they need to think about.
3. If the answer stays vague after that, treat it as a likely soft no. Move on;
   don't chase.

The reframe is the same in both tracks: never push past a real no, help them get
to a clear one.

## Mentor's team-building sequence

Get to MG1 → get qualified for launch → grow the team one relationship at a
time from there.

Team incentive trips are the model once a front-line team of ~5 exists (past:
lakefront property; future target: Puerto Rico).

## Logging

After every real candidate conversation, append to `../../memory.md` under
"Partner track": the candidate, the step they're on now, what landed, what
stalled. Before, not after, is a prediction — don't log those.

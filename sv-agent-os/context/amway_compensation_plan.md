## The two currencies: PV and BV

Every Amway product carries two numbers, and confusing them is the single most common
mistake when explaining the plan.

| | What it is | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **PV** (Point Value) | A unitless point score | Determines *which bonus percentage bracket* you land in |
| **BV** (Business Volume) | A dollar figure | The base the bonus percentage is *applied to* |

So: **PV picks your rate. BV is what the rate multiplies.**

A rough US ratio has historically been around BV ≈ 3× PV, but treat that as a sanity check,
not a fact — look it up.

## The four ways money reaches you

### 1. Retail margin

You buy at IBO cost and sell at retail. The difference is yours immediately. This is the
most straightforward income in the business and the part that requires no team at all.

```
Retail margin = (retail price − your IBO cost) × units sold
```

### 2. Performance Bonus on your own volume

Your total monthly **group PV** (yours plus your team's) determines a percentage. That
percentage is paid on your own **BV**.

```
Performance Bonus = your BV × bracket%(group PV)
```

### 3. Differential on your team's volume

This is the part people miss, and it is the whole engine of the "ten people selling ten"
scenario.

Each person on your team qualifies for their *own* bracket based on *their* volume. You
qualify for a *higher* bracket because your group PV includes all of theirs. You are paid
the **difference** between your percentage and theirs, on their BV.

```
Differential = their BV × (your bracket% − their bracket%)
```

**This is why the team scenario is not simply "11× one person."** Adding volume does two
things at once: it adds a differential stream, *and* it lifts your own percentage into a
higher bracket. With the placeholder numbers, one person at 10 units/month sits in the
12% bracket; that same person with ten others doing the same is in the 25% bracket. The
jump in bracket is the story, not the extra sales.

### 4. Customer sales incentives and higher awards

Amway layers additional bonuses on top — customer-volume incentives, leadership bonuses,
and awards at higher pin levels. **These are deliberately excluded from the calculator.**
They have qualification requirements that a new person will not meet, and including them
inflates the illustration in exactly the way that gets people in trouble. Add them only
after you have verified the rules and the person is actually close to qualifying.

## Worked example (placeholder rates)

Assumptions: eSpring retail $1,400, IBO cost $1,180, 106 PV, $330 BV.

**Just you — 10 units/month**

```
Retail margin      10 × $220              = $2,200
Your PV            10 × 106               = 1,060 PV  → 12% bracket
Performance Bonus  10 × $330 × 12%        =   $396
                                          ─────────
                                            $2,596 / month
```

**You + 10 people each selling 10**

```
Retail margin      10 × $220                        = $2,200
Group PV           1,060 + (10 × 1,060) = 11,660 PV → 25% bracket
Own bonus          $3,300 BV × 25%                  =   $825
Differential       10 legs × $3,300 BV × (25%−12%)  = $4,290
                                                     ─────────
                                                       $7,315 / month
```

Note that the team scenario is **2.8×** the solo one, not 11×. Your team keeps their own
12%; you earn the 13-point spread. Anyone who tells a prospect it multiplies by eleven is
either confused or lying, and the prospect will find out.

# Skill: Customer Referrals

**Use this when:** asking an existing eSpring customer for a referral, or handling
one that comes in unprompted.

**Done looks like:** a named person with a reason attached — "my sister just
bought in Ooltewah and the water tastes like the pool" — and the customer's
agreement to make the introduction. A vague "I'll think of someone" is not a
referral.

## The one timing rule

**Never ask before a positive experience check.** The day 14 message in
`lifecycle_automation.md` is the gate. A referral ask from someone whose water
hasn't changed yet is asking them to vouch for something they haven't verified.

Ask once. If the answer is soft, drop it and let the day 90 check-in or a later
positive moment create the next opening. Same posture as everywhere else in this
business: never push past a real no.

## Two kinds of referral

### 1. People — friends and family

The natural ask, and the one that needs no verification.

> "Who else do you know on [their utility / in their neighborhood] who'd want to
> know what you found out about the water?"

Better than "do you know anyone who'd want one," because it points at a specific
group and asks about the water rather than the product. Follow-ups that work:

- Family in the same water system.
- Neighbors — same utility, same report, same problem.
- Anyone they've already told about it. Someone who's retold the story once will
  retell it again.

The customer makes the introduction; we don't cold-contact a name they handed
over. Ask how they'd like to do it — text intro, group text, or just a heads-up
that we'll reach out.

### 2. Businesses — needs a verification gate first

> **Compliance gate.** The eSpring Owner's Manual restricts use to **residential
> and light commercial (office break rooms)**. **Hotels and restaurants are
> excluded.** Coffee shops, restaurants, and bars — the businesses customers most
> naturally think of — fall in the excluded category.

So the "a business I wish had one" idea only runs in its compliant form:

**Can be pitched now:** offices and break rooms — the small office, the
contractor's shop, the real estate office, the church office, the gym's staff
area. Single decision-maker, whole-building thinking. This overlaps the segment
list in `../../reps/skills/sales_script.md`.

**Cannot be pitched without verification:** anything food-service or
hospitality — restaurants, coffee shops, bars, hotels. Route these to a
verification step:

1. Log the business, the customer who suggested it, and why.
2. Verify the intended use with Amway **before** any contact.
3. Only after written confirmation does it become a live target.

Nothing gets pitched to an excluded business on the assumption it'll probably be
fine. If verification says no, tell the customer plainly — "that one's outside
what the product's approved for" — and redirect the ask to their home and office
network. A customer who suggested it deserves a real answer, and the honesty
protects the referral relationship.

The commercial ask itself:

> "Is there anywhere you spend time — an office, a shop, somewhere you work — where
> you've thought the water could be better?"

Phrased around places they spend time rather than businesses they like, it lands
on offices naturally instead of collecting a list of restaurants we can't serve.

## What the referrer gets

**Open decision — not set.** Before any referral incentive is offered, it has to
clear two things: Amway's rules on customer incentives, and the no-income-claims
rule. Until that's resolved, the ask is made on the strength of the water story
alone, with no reward promised.

Do not invent an incentive to make an ask land better.

## Handling an inbound referral

1. Thank the referring customer and tell them what happened — people who hear back
   refer again; people who don't, don't.
2. Open with the connection, not the product: "[Customer] mentioned you're on the
   same water system."
3. Run the normal eSpring conversation from
   `../../reps/skills/sales_script.md` — a referral warms the opener, it doesn't
   replace the process. Still question-led, still demo, still the dip test.
4. Log the outcome under "Customer track" in `../../memory.md`, including which
   ask produced it. Over time that's the answer to which referral question
   actually works.

## The upgrade signal

A customer who refers repeatedly is telling you something. Someone who has sent
three people is already selling — that's a **rep** candidate
(`../../reps/context/icp.md`). Have that conversation separately, on its own day,
never inside a service follow-up.

# Skill: Connecting — Conversations, Make Aware, Door to Mentorship

**Use this when:** working the Names List, writing or practicing a Make Aware
message, opening the door to mentorship, or scripting a call back. Everything
downstream of the call back is in `qualification_process.md`.

**Done looks like:** a specific person moved one notch along
`Conversation → MA → DTM → Call Back → MG1`, and it's logged on the Core Run
tracker. A conversation where you never found out whether they're looking hasn't
moved anyone.

**Source:** the Yager Group / InterNET Services *Qualification Process*
document, "Guide to Create Your Business Connections." Scripts below are from
that document — memorize them, don't improvise them.

## The roadmap

```
Conversations → MA → DTM → (Call Back) → MG1 → MG2 → OV1 → FT1 → OV2 → FT2 → Offer
```

Ask in-depth questions about dreams and goals; establish credibility to meet your
mentor. If you're newly launched and in your first 30/60/90-day Core Run,
practice with your mentor or coach before running these live.

## The Names List

Written list, 250+ names, including social media contacts. **Don't contact
anyone yet.** Segment it:

- **P** — potential partner (team) candidates
- **C** — potential customers

The Names List is your business capital.

## Make Aware (MA)

Casually informing someone of this business mentorship. Target: **20–30 seconds**.
The message's job is to find out whether they're looking — not to explain the
business.

### People you know (inner circle)

Re-establish the relationship first. Talk **FORM**: Family, Occupation,
Recreation, Mentorship.

- "I didn't want to say anything until I got accepted, but I recently qualified
  through a selection process to work with a private business mentor and I'm
  really learning a lot. I think it will make a huge difference in my life."
- "I've actually been plugged into this mentor the last few weeks/months,
  learning about entrepreneurship / creating income producing assets / how to
  budget my money to create success."
- "(…work, activities) but what I am really excited about is what I am learning
  from my mentors."

### People you meet

Don't overthink it. Take genuine interest, ask lots of questions, listen
actively, and lead the conversation. Ask about their profession, say briefly what
you do, and mention you're excited about qualifying for a private mentor
relationship and learning to build a debt-free, ongoing cash flow **asset**.

Conversation starters: "Have you worked here long? Do you enjoy it?" · "I love
your shoes, where did you get them?" · "Nice tie, my mentor would like that!" ·
"I detect an accent, where are you from?" · "Do you live around here?" · "What do
you do for a living?" · "Do you know a good restaurant nearby?" · anything FORM.

### The Make Aware message structure

- **The Who** — "A while back I was fortunate enough to get introduced to a couple
  of entrepreneurs."
- **The How** — "I found out they are private mentors. Long story short, after a
  few conversations, I qualified / was selected to work with them."
- **The What** — "Now, they are coaching and mentoring me to develop my own
  assets…"
- **The Why** — "…so that I can eventually replace my job income and spend my time
  doing the things I want to do."

Write it out and memorize it as your answer to "What do you do?":

- "Well, right now I'm ______ but what I'm really excited about this year is that
  I've been able to link up with some successful businesspeople who are mentoring
  me. They've taught a number of people how to set up income producing assets."
- "My background is ______ but my focus is with a business mentorship group
  outside my 9 to 5."
- "My background is ______ but my focus lies in building some business assets on
  the side with a mentorship group."

> If there is no interest in mentorship or building assets, move on with the
> conversation.

Often there's no response at all to the MA. You can ask whether they've ever had
a mentor, keep developing the connection, and find a way to stay in touch.

### Questions that open people up

Appropriate before or after the MA:

- "Have you ever had a mentor?"
- "Do you have any other income sources?"
- "Do you do anything to diversify?"
- "What entrepreneurial experience have you had?"
- "What have you done outside of your profession?"
- "Have you ever read *Rich Dad, Poor Dad*?"
- "Have you ever thought about being your own boss?"
- "How do you see people getting ahead?"

> If they don't show interest, drop the subject; continue the relationship if
> there's value in it.

### Answering "What is it?" / "What kind of assets?"

Memorize a simple response. Anything past one question is more detail than a
casual conversation should carry.

- "They have had success in multiple industries."
- "We are learning how to develop financial assets of our own."
- "Assets with cash flow."

If they push further:

- "At this point I'm just getting started in the education part, so I don't know
  enough to explain the process."
- "To be honest I'm just learning right now."
- "You know what, that's a 45-minute conversation over a cup of coffee."
- "You know, I'm really just a student myself right now."
- "That would be a great question for my mentor."

## Door to Mentorship (DTM)

Only open the door when you genuinely believe they're looking and may be a good
candidate — and only after you've found their need, desire, or purpose for more
in life. Relate to what **they** said they want.

Bridge questions:

- "If I could mention your name to them, what kind of value would you place on
  that?"
- "Occasionally they take on another person. Would it be of value to you if I
  check on it for you?"
- "From what you've said, it sounds like getting ahead is important to you. If I
  could get you connected to my mentor, would that be of value to you?"

**If the response is highly positive:**

- "I'll be talking to my mentors soon. Text me your number and I'll let you know
  what I find out. What's one thing about yourself that you would want them to
  know about you?"
- "I can't promise you anything, but let me check with my mentor to see if
  they're taking on any more people. I'll text you my number and get back to you
  within a few days."

**If they say not now / no:**

- Compliment them and put them on a list to follow up in **6 months**.
- "Sounds like you know where you're headed. Let's stay in touch, I'd love to
  hear how it goes for you."
- "No problem, let's just stay in touch."

**Picking it back up later** — if you made someone aware but never opened the
door, you can return to it:

> "When we last spoke I was telling you about the mentors I got connected with. I
> found out they may be doing some selection interviews soon. What value would it
> be to you if I could try to make a connection for you?"

## Call Back

**If you're running your own Meet & Greets:**

> "My mentor asked a couple of things about you that I didn't know how to answer.
> He suggested I meet with you. Would you like to meet up for coffee and go from
> there?"

**If you're running MGs with your mentor:**

> "Good news! I put a good word in for you. My mentor agreed for me to introduce
> you to him. He suggested we meet for coffee — what does your calendar look
> like?"

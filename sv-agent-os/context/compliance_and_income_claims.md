# Compliance & Income Claims

**Read this before showing anyone an income number.** Getting this wrong risks your
Amway business, not just a bad meeting.

## The one rule

Any time you present income figures to a prospect, you must present the current
**Amway Independent Business Owner Compensation Plan / Income Disclosure Statement**
alongside them. It reports what IBOs actually earn across the whole business, including
the large share who earn little or nothing.

This applies to:

- The calculator in `tools/`
- Whiteboard math
- Screenshots of your own bonus checks
- "My upline makes X" stories
- Anything numeric, written or spoken

## Why this matters beyond the rulebook

The FTC regulates earnings claims in business-opportunity settings. The standard is not
"was it technically possible" — it is whether the presentation would mislead a reasonable
person about what they are likely to earn. A hypothetical that a prospect hears as a
forecast is a misleading claim even if the arithmetic is correct.

So the framing matters as much as the disclosure:

| Say this | Not this |
|---|---|
| "This is how the plan calculates income at this volume." | "This is what you'll make." |
| "This assumes you sell ten every month, which most people don't." | "It's only ten a month." |
| "Here's the disclosure statement showing actual averages." | *(no disclosure shown)* |
| "Most people earn modest amounts or nothing." | *(omitted)* |

## What makes the calculator's numbers hypothetical

Be able to say these out loud. If you can't defend the assumptions, don't show the number.

1. **Every unit sells, every month.** The model assumes ten sales a month, indefinitely.
   Sales are not steady, and eSpring is a high-ticket, considered purchase.
2. **Team members perform identically.** Ten people each selling ten, every month, is an
   assumption about ten other humans — not something you control.
3. **No attrition.** Real teams lose people. The model has no churn.
4. **No costs.** Product samples, events, travel, tools, system fees, and your own time
   are not subtracted. Net is lower than gross, sometimes much lower.
5. **All volume qualifies.** Assumes every requirement for the bonus bracket is met.
6. **Rates are current.** Amway's plan and product pricing change.

The honest version of the pitch names these. Say the assumptions before the prospect
finds them, and the number you *do* show carries more weight.

## Language to avoid entirely

- "Guaranteed", "passive income", "retire in X months"
- "Risk-free", "can't lose"
- Any specific promise about *this* person's results
- Implying the illustrated figure is typical or average

## Pre-meeting checklist

- [ ] Every rate in the calculator verified against current official Amway sources
- [ ] Confirm box ticked in the Plan Rates panel (clears the warning banner)
- [ ] Current Income Disclosure Statement printed or on-screen, ready to hand over
- [ ] I can state all six assumptions above from memory
- [ ] I am prepared to say "most people earn modest amounts, and many earn nothing"
